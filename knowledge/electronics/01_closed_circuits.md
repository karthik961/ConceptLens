# Closed paths, open circuits, and bulbs

**Concept notes written for the ConceptLens starter demo; not a reference to any BEETLE answer or label.**

A simple battery–wire–bulb circuit lights the bulb when a complete conducting path connects the two battery terminals through the bulb. Current can flow around this closed loop, and the bulb converts electrical energy into light and heat.

Opening a switch creates a gap in a path and interrupts current in that path. The bulb goes out because the conducting loop is broken; the fact that it is dark does not, by itself, mean the battery's stored chemical energy has been exhausted.

A switch does not normally consume all the charge in the battery. It changes whether the current path is complete. A battery can still maintain a potential difference across its terminals while the circuit is open, although no steady current flows through an ideal open switch.
