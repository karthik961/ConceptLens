# Voltage, current, battery and resistance

**Concept notes written for the ConceptLens starter demo.**

Voltage is electric potential difference between two points, measured in volts. A battery uses chemical reactions to maintain an electrical potential difference between its terminals.

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes. Current through a component requires a conducting path and a driving potential difference. Charge is not normally used up at a component; energy is transferred.

Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes current under specified conditions. For an ohmic resistor, the relationship between voltage V, current I, and resistance R is V = I R.

A voltage reading between two points is not the same thing as the current flowing through a device. A voltmeter connects across the two points; an ammeter is commonly inserted in series with the branch of interest.
